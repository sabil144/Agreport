Hormuz Stays Shut. Urea Doesn’t Care — Phosphates Do.

By Commodex Intelligence Desk

Chinese urea exports have broken the nitrogen squeeze, but phosphate and sulphur remain punishing and Gulf-origin cargo still cannot transit. Urea is looking more controllable as the market broadens beyond the Persian Gulf, but the risk premium in phosphates remains elevated.

Key takeaways

  • Urea: China export quota and Indian tender bids have softened the front end, even though gulf product remains effectively unavailable.
  • Phosphate: DAP and TSP continue to reflect sulphur and China export restrictions; the issue is not crude, it is feedstock and supply access.
  • Shipping: Hormuz remains effectively closed, leaving cargo origin and route choice as the main decision variable.
The main practical lesson is simple: because urea is more available from China and SE Asia, buyers should lock shorter-dated nitrogen supply while treating Gulf cargo as inaccessible until the transit environment is visibly better.

Market read-through

For importers, the current picture still rewards contract diversification. The best exposed contracts are in DAP/TSP, where sulphur and Chinese export policy dominate. For urea, the market is more balanced, but route risk and delivered price must still be checked against real cargo availability.

Action

Favour Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt for non-urea nutrient sourcing, and continue to treat Saudi, Qatar, UAE and Oman origin as blocked unless a confirmed route is available. Use China/SE Asia as the primary urea route while locking hedged price windows where the landed CFR remains within current budget levels.
