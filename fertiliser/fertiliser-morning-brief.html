01

Urea — Nitrogen

China's export tap loosens, price gives back the risk premium

Middle East prilled benchmark eased to $443/t on 4 September, down slightly on the day but still +13.6% month‑on‑month and +13.2% year‑on‑year. Beijing expanded its 2026 export allowance to roughly 3.3 million tonnes (2.97mt state trading + 0.33mt non‑state) — down from ~5mt in 2025 — concentrated into a narrow June–August window, with July shipments jumping to ~403,000t from just 7,000t in June.

China is running a dual‑lever policy: rationing volume while setting FOB price floors — $430/t prilled and $440/t granular for non‑India business, and a steeper $500/t floor for India‑bound cargo. Regional spreads are wide: September benchmarks range from $380–385/t (Black Sea prilled) to $502.5/t (Egypt large granular).

Demand-side: India's RCF tender absorbed 1.78mt of bids below $400/t; an earlier NFL tender drew 6.25mt of bids against a 1.7mt target, underscoring how import‑dependent buyers are still chasing scarce cargo even as headline prices soften.

02

DAP · TSP · Phosphate

China's curbs extended, sulphur costs stack on top

China's phosphate export restrictions (DAP, MAP, select NPK) — first imposed mid‑March 2026 after the Hormuz disruption began — were extended through August 2026, now covering 50–80% of China's fertiliser export volumes. Russia is separately capping outbound nitrogen and phosphate tonnage via the Eurasian Economic Union framework. Ammonium sulphate remains comparatively less restricted.

US domestic reference prices ticked up again in the week to 18 September: DAP $925/t, MAP $967/t, both higher week‑on‑week and part of a run that has taken Indian DAP import costs up roughly 40% between January and May. India's DAP imports over Jan–Apr fell to just 380,000t versus a five‑year average of 1.12mt — the shortfall, not the price alone, is the story.

Sulphur, the key phosphate feedstock, has reportedly spiked sharply this year as Gulf sulphur recovery streams are disrupted, forcing some phosphate producers to trim output — a secondary cost pressure layered on top of the freight and export‑quota squeeze.

03

Potash

The quiet nutrient — steady, but riding the same freight tax as everything else

US domestic MOP reference held near $495/t for the week to 18 September, up marginally from $494/t the prior week — the least volatile of the eight majors DTN tracks, with no move of 5% or more in either direction. Potash is largely insulated from the Hormuz chokepoint itself (Canadian, Belarusian and Russian supply chains don't transit the strait), but broader commentary — including from the World Bank — groups potash into the general "fertiliser prices surge on Hormuz disruption" narrative via shared ocean freight and insurance cost inflation across the fertiliser complex.

No fresh potash‑specific tender or contract news out of the Gulf or South/Southeast Asia in the past 24–48 hours; treat this as the stable leg of the complex for now.

04

Bangladesh & Australia

Bangladesh: financed, but structurally short on Gulf routes

ITFC signed a $3.3bn annual financing programme with Bangladesh (5 July 2026, covering FY2026–27) for combined petroleum, LNG and fertiliser imports as Hormuz disruption deepens Dhaka's energy and food security exposure. Context: 1.4mt/yr of crude normally transiting Hormuz is blocked, 75% of Bangladesh's LNG (from Qatar) is largely halted, and ~35% of Bangladesh's urea imports historically came from the UAE — now rerouted or renegotiated.

A Saudi‑Bangladesh arrangement has SABIC supplying urea via alternative routing for 2026–27, and Dhaka's cabinet committee has approved further G2G urea import deals for FY27. March–July is Bangladesh's critical Boro/Kharif application window, so timing risk on any delayed cargo remains the key operational threat, not headline price.

Australia: EFA underwriting widens, farmers still feeling the pinch

Export Finance Australia's mandate was expanded (legislation amended 1 April 2026) to underwrite fuel and fertiliser imports disrupted by lost Gulf feedstock and shipping, alongside a new critical‑minerals strategic reserve (antimony, gallium, rare earths). Australia separately secured 90,000t of urea through a government support scheme by mid‑May.

More recent trade commentary suggests the Fuel and Fertiliser Security Facility is drawing criticism from some domestic fertiliser companies who say the support isn't reaching them as intended — a friction point worth watching if it prompts programme changes or a fresh call for import volume.

Sourcing Watch — Gulf / North Africa → Australia & Bangladesh

Relevant to urea/TSP/DAP sourcing from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, UAE, Morocco, Tunisia and Oman into Australia and Bangladesh:

Oman · Duqm reroute

Maaden is actively loading Saudi DAP through Duqm (bypassing Hormuz and the Red Sea) — $15–20/t freight saving to India/East Africa. This is the cleanest current workaround lane for Gulf‑origin phosphate; worth checking Duqm slot availability for AU/BD cargo.

UAE · urea, rerouted

~35% of Bangladesh's historical urea supply came from the UAE. SABIC (Saudi) is stepping into that gap for 2026–27 under a bilateral deal — a signal that direct UAE-origin urea contracts may face continued routing friction.

Morocco · OCP gaining share

OCP is a net beneficiary of Gulf disruption, absorbing displaced phosphate demand. Non‑Hormuz origin makes Morocco a lower‑risk TSP/DAP counterparty for both Australian and Bangladeshi buyers right now, though OCP maintenance schedules are a watch item.

Qatar · LNG-linked exposure

Qatari LNG production curtailment (feeding Bangladesh's gas/urea feedstock chain) is the bigger indirect risk here versus direct fertiliser export — watch feedstock gas availability at Qatari nitrogen plants.

Tunisia · capacity building, not yet a swing supplier

GCT is investing in phosphate output/exports under its 2026–2030 plan and drew fresh financing this year, but Hormuz‑linked input disruption is still constraining near‑term production — a 2027+ story more than a spot‑cargo one today.

Saudi Arabia · formula pricing, Duqm-first

Saudi (Maaden/SABIC) cargo is increasingly formula‑priced and Duqm‑routed rather than spot Hormuz transits. Direct Hormuz transits of Saudi phosphate remain rare — only two confirmed since hostilities began in late February.
