Executive Insight

Diesel, not crude, is the cost that hits harvest first

Brent at US$106 is the headline, but the transmission into our P&L runs through distillate: US diesel is at a record, Russia's export ban runs to 31 October and Australian terminal gate prices are tracking Singapore gasoil higher into the busiest fuel-burn weeks of the year.

Brent +17.5% m/mUS diesel $6.53/galTGP ≈255–270 c/L est.AUD/USD 0.7018Hormuz 7.4 of 20m b/d

A Hormuz deal would cut crude within 48 hours; refilling the world's distillate tanks takes weeks. That asymmetry is why fuel and freight fuel clauses should be fixed now, not after the headline.

Recommended action

  1. Fix Oct–Dec diesel volumes on capped pricing this week.
  2. Fix Oct/Nov Pacific bulk tonnage with explicit BAF clauses.
  3. Reset road/rail FAF to a TGP-indexed formula before October.
  4. Layer USD hedges on any post-RBA AUD bounce toward 0.71.
If we only remember one thing from today's report

Diesel, not crude, is the cost that will hit harvest logistics first and hardest — lock October–December fuel and freight fuel clauses now, because a Hormuz deal would cut Brent fast but would take weeks to refill the world's distillate tanks.
