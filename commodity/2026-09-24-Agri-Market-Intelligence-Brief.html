The Brief · 7 Signals
🇦🇺 Australia Supply Bearish basis

ABARES lifts winter crop to 53.6Mt — big upward revisions across the board

ABARES raised its national winter crop forecast to 53.6Mt: wheat 29.9Mt (+12% on June), barley 16.4Mt (+16%) and canola 7.27Mt (+17%). A wetter-than-expected south-east and softer fertiliser drag did the lifting; SA and Vic are running 3–4 weeks ahead, while northern NSW and Qld stay poor.

👉 Implication: A larger exportable surplus pressures domestic basis into harvest — lean bearish on old-crop longs and price new-crop wheat/barley on rallies rather than holding for scarcity.
Source: Grain Central · 1 Sep 2026
🇷🇺 Russia / 🇺🇦 Ukraine Policy · Black Sea Bearish wheat

Russia zeroes export duties through December, adds 3Mt intervention buying

Russia scrapped export duties on wheat, barley and corn through December and flagged a 3Mt intervention purchase program; Russian cash wheat sat flat at US$210. Ukraine wrapped its season at 33Mt wheat with barley exports down 41% YoY.

👉 Implication: Cheap, duty-free Russian supply caps world wheat and undercuts Australian FOB into Asia/MENA — a headwind for holding wheat length; sell into any Black-Sea-driven spikes.
Source: Grain Central — Daily Market Wire · 23 Sep 2026
🇵🇰 Pakistan Demand · Imports Bullish demand

Pakistan turns wheat importer — 750kt tender, then a fresh 185kt float

Facing thin farmer selling at harvest, Pakistan's TCP tendered for up to 750,000t of wheat (nine bids for 656,000t, lowest at US$348.83/t) and floated a further 185,000t tender on 21 Sep. It's a regional buyer re-emerging mid-season.

👉 Implication: New subcontinent import demand is a rare bullish offset for wheat — watch whether Australian/Black Sea origins win cargoes; supportive for keeping some export-grade wheat unpriced.
Source: Express Tribune · The Nation · 9–21 Sep 2026
🇨🇦 Canada Oilseeds · Supply Hold canola

Record Canadian canola meets a friendlier China and firm biofuel pull

Canada's canola acreage hit a historic record for 2026, and China's earlier cut to its anti-dumping duty on Canadian seed has reopened that channel. Structural biofuel demand keeps a floor under the complex even as ample supply builds.

👉 Implication: Big Canadian tonnage competes head-on with Australian canola into the EU/Asia, but biodiesel demand limits downside — favour hold over aggressive selling and use crude-led dips to add cover.
Source: Top Crop Manager · RealAgriculture
🌐 Global oilseeds Prices Buy dips

Canola retreats with crude — but the biofuel bid stays intact

Canola followed energy lower: WCE Nov26 −A$11.63 to A$828.28 and Matif Nov26 −A$7.37 to A$892.25, even as local WA bids firmed to A$888 (east A$830). Matif maize jumped €7.25 to €276, now €32 over Matif wheat.

👉 Implication: The pullback is crude-driven, not demand-driven. For Australian CAN1 sellers, treat the dip as tactical — scale-price rather than dump; strong local bids signal exporters still chasing tonnes.
Source: Grain Central — Daily Market Wire · 23 Sep 2026
🇮🇳 India Policy · Pulses Bearish pulses

India keeps the import taps open — duty-free peas & urad to March 2027

India extended duty-free imports of yellow peas and black gram (urad) to 31 March 2027, scrapping minimum-price and port limits on peas, while chickpeas still carry a 10% duty. Desi chickpea CNF has been easing toward US$450–470/t on heavy global supply.

👉 Implication: Cheap substitute imports cap Indian pulse values and pressure Australian desi chickpea/faba prices — forward-sell into firm windows and lean on lentils, where South-Asian demand holds better.
Source: Down To Earth · UkrAgroConsult
💱 Forex Macro · FX Margin watch

AUD/USD steady at 0.7115 as RBA–Fed tug-of-war holds the range

The Aussie held 0.7115 against a soft US dollar, keeping it in the upper part of its multi-month band. A firmer AUD trims the local-currency value of USD-denominated grain and pulse sales.

👉 Implication: With AUD in the upper range, consider layering forward FX cover on unsold export tonnage; a break higher on Fed easing would compress margins further — hedge, don't hope.

Special · Pulse Trade Cross-Check

Pulses: subcontinent demand vs record Southern-Hemisphere supply

Australia · India · Pakistan · Bangladesh — checked against Canadian competition and crop production.

🇦🇺 Australia

Heading into another large pulse crop (chickpeas ~2.5Mt class, big lentil program). India and Pakistan are the anchor buyers; desi chickpea and faba values are softening as global supply builds, while lentils hold firmer on steadier South-Asian pull.

🇮🇳 India

Policy is the market-mover: duty-free yellow peas & urad to Mar-2027, 10% chickpea duty retained. Cheap peas substitute for chana, capping desi chickpea prices; importers eye CNF $450–470/t. Bearish tilt for supplier netbacks.

🇵🇰 Pakistan / 🇧🇩 Bangladesh

Pakistan is short — importing wheat and a steady chickpea/lentil buyer; lower Indian prices drag Pakistani values too. Bangladesh remains a reliable lentil/pea destination, a useful diversification outlet away from India-only risk.

🇨🇦 Canada — the competitor

Record canola and a large pulse program (peas, lentils) keep Canada shipping aggressively into the same Asian lanes. Its harvest finishes ahead of Australia's, so Canadian offers set the early price floor buyers quote back to Aussie exporters.

Net read for the desk

Supply is winning the narrative: record Australian and Canadian output + open Indian import policy = bearish pressure on desi chickpeas and peas. Lentils are the relative safe harbour on durable South-Asian demand. Action: forward-sell chickpeas/faba into strength, diversify beyond India toward Pakistan & Bangladesh, and watch for any Indian chickpea-duty change as the key bullish swing factor.
